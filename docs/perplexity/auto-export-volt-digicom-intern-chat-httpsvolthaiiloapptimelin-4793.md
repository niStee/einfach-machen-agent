This chat is essentially a classic GDPR-vs-operational-security tension, and there are clear answers on multiple fronts. Here's a breakdown of what's legally correct, what's practically viable, and where the proposals in the chat land:

The Core GDPR Problem

Routinely requiring ID copies is legally risky. The Dutch DPA (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens) explicitly ruled against it in the DPG Media case, finding it disproportionate. German law similarly restricts storing ID copies — controllers generally need a strong specific legal basis, and a logged confirmation that "identity was verified" is usually sufficient. GDPR Recital 64 demands reasonable measures, not perfect certainty, and explicitly prohibits retaining personal data solely for potential future requests.

Cleverbase Is the Most Realistic Option

Cleverbase is already integrated with the EU Digital Identity (EUDI) Wallet framework and was used in a Dutch municipal pilot for issuing verified credentials. It's eIDAS-qualified, used by ING and NVM brokers, and provides a digital wallet where identity attributes (name, DOB, document info) can be shared with qualified consent — meaning the user controls what's shared and nothing is stored by Volt. At €3.75/identity, onboarding 100 new team members/candidates would cost €375. That's absolutely within reach.

For the Infra Team Access Case Specifically

The scenario Antoine raised (onboarding someone to access 38,000 member records) warrants a higher bar than a board candidate, but the answer is still not a passport copy. The proportionate approach:

Video call with two existing trusted members who confirm identity and sign a log entry

NDA + Supporter/Volunteer agreement (already exists per the chat)

Phased access under supervision before full access is granted

Optionally: Cleverbase for a one-time eIDAS-level check if the sensitivity justifies the cost

The two-person attestation model is common in security-sensitive onboarding and fully GDPR-compliant — it's essentially what Volt's electoral commission should have been doing for board candidates all along.

On BundID/DigiD as Long-Term Vision

The suggestion to use national eID systems is forward-looking but currently blocked for NGO use. The EU Digital Identity Wallet regulation (eIDAS 2.0) will eventually make cross-border, NGO-accessible identity verification standard — Cleverbase is already building on this stack. That's likely the right long-term direction, but Volt shouldn't wait for it. A video call policy + Cleverbase for high-stakes onboarding is deployable now.
